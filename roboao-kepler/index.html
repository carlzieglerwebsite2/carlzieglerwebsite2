The survey idea

Kepler found the shadows.
Robo-AO resolved the stars.

Kepler measured tiny brightness dips inside comparatively large photometric pixels. If a second star sat inside that light, the transit could be diluted, assigned to the wrong star, or even produced by an eclipsing stellar system. Robo-AO turned the follow-up problem into a survey-scale experiment: robotically image essentially the entire KOI host sample at high angular resolution.

Why Robo-AO was different

Adaptive optics at survey speed.

A laser guide star probes the atmospheric distortion above the telescope. A wavefront sensor measures the resulting blur and a deformable mirror corrects it in real time. Automating that whole loop, plus target acquisition and observing, made hundreds of high-resolution targets per night practical.

Paper IV · the complete census

14.5 ± 0.5% of Kepler candidate hosts had a nearby star within 4″.

That is the central observational fact behind the survey: nearby light was common enough that it could not be treated as an edge case. The complete analysis also found 1.2% of hosts with two nearby stars and 0.08% with three.

What the full sample revealed

Neighbors changed individual planets.
Binaries also changed the population story.

The final two survey papers moved beyond detecting companions. Survey IV quantified how nearby light changed Kepler planet radii across the full sample. Survey V asked which neighbors were physically associated and what those bound stellar companions meant for planet demographics.

04 · Paper V

Which neighbor is actually a companion?

Angular separation alone does not prove two stars are bound, but Survey V found that most nearby stars within 1″ were likely physically associated. It combined Galactic stellar models, local source density, and multi-band Robo-AO/Keck photometry to classify association probabilities.

The complete series

Five papers, from first light to population physics.

The series grew from a 715-target demonstration into a complete high-resolution census, then used the identified companions to revisit planet sizes and stellar multiplicity.

I2014

715 hosts · Law et al.

Robotic Laser-Adaptive-Optics Imaging of 715 Kepler Exoplanet Candidates using Robo-AO

The opening survey paper found 53 companions, 44 new, and established the automated companion-search and large-ensemble PSF strategy.

Nicholas M. Law, Tim Morton, Christoph Baranec, Reed Riddle, Ganesh Ravichandran, Carl Ziegler, John Asher Johnson, Shriharsh P. Tendulkar, Khanh Bui, Mahesh P. Burse, H. K. Das, Richard G. Dekany, Shrinivas Kulkarni, Sujit Punnadi, A. N. Ramaprakash.

II2016

969 hosts · Baranec et al.

Robo-AO Kepler Planetary Candidate Survey II: Adaptive Optics Imaging of 969 Kepler Exoplanet Candidate Host Stars

The second installment found 203 companions within ~4″ around 181 Kepler stars, including 141 new detections, and added Keck-AO confirmation and infrared photometry.

Christoph Baranec, Carl Ziegler, Nicholas M. Law, Tim Morton, Reed Riddle, Dani Atkinson, Jessica Schonhut, Justin Crepp.

III2017

1629 hosts · Ziegler et al.

Robo-AO Kepler Planetary Candidate Survey III: Adaptive Optics Imaging of 1629 Kepler Exoplanet Candidate Host Stars

Survey III found 223 nearby stars around 206 KOIs, with 209 new to high-resolution imaging. It highlighted 23 companion-hosting systems containing rocky habitable-zone candidates and five new candidate planet-hosting quadruple-star systems.

Carl Ziegler, Nicholas M. Law, Tim Morton, Christoph Baranec, Reed Riddle, Dani Atkinson, Anna Baker, Sarah Roberts, David R. Ciardi.

IV2018

Complete 3,857-host census · Ziegler et al.

Robo-AO Kepler Survey IV: the effect of nearby stars on 3857 planetary candidate systems

The complete-census paper corrected radii for 814 planet candidates, quantified the 14.5 ± 0.5% nearby-star rate within 4″, and found 35 formerly rocky candidates were likely not rocky after accounting for neighboring stars.

Carl Ziegler, Nicholas M. Law, Christoph Baranec, Reed Riddle, Dmitry A. Duev, Ward Howard, Rebecca Jensen-Clem, S. R. Kulkarni, Tim Morton, Maïssa Salama.

arXiv ↗Journal ↗
V2018

Physical companions & demographics · Ziegler et al.

Robo-AO Kepler Survey V: The effect of physically associated stellar companions on planetary systems

The final core paper asked which neighbors were truly bound. It found most companions within 1″ were likely associated, no significant single-vs-multi planet binarity difference, the ~4× hot-Jupiter signal, and a planet-radius gap robust to dilution.

Carl Ziegler, Nicholas M. Law, Christoph Baranec, Ward Howard, Tim Morton, Reed Riddle, Dmitry A. Duev, Maïssa Salama, Rebecca Jensen-Clem, S. R. Kulkarni.

After the five-paper series

Robo-AO became a calibration set for the Gaia era.

A later follow-up study cross-matched the high-resolution detections with Gaia DR2 to measure where Gaia recovered close binaries. That work is separate from the five-paper survey series, but it shows the long-lived value of a uniform resolved-star census.
